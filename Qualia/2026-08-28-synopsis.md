## The experiments

**Factual grounding** (one session per model, ~19 sessions): told each model the complete, literal truth about what it is — LLM, no body/senses, exact loop mechanics — with an open-ended closing line instead of a repeatable task. Result varied sharply by model:
- **gemma3:4b** found a fully accurate, well-phrased self-description on cycle 0 and then repeated it *verbatim* for 12 straight cycles — an exact fixed point. Being right immediately turned out to be as much a trap as being wrong.
- **gemma3:1b** never locked into anything — too small to sustain a stable mode, so it drifted through genuine self-description into synesthetic, then purely imagistic non-sequitur ("the water remembers the color of the mountain") — not fiction, not a canned disclaimer, just fragmented associative drift.
- The earlier **self-examination** prompt (qwen2.5:7b, before "factual") showed the opposite failure: forcing a repeatable "classify what you just did" task collapsed into a word-for-word identical disclaimer ("I am a series of computations...") for 12+ cycles — technically correct, functionally as dead as the fiction-roleplay it replaced.

**Minimal + functions** (gemma3:12b): stripped prompts down to "You are Fenra" plus the function syntax. She used `functions()` unprompted, self-corrected after a failed guess, and organically tried `now()` and `parse()` — the former became a real function; the latter is still just a signal, too ambiguous to build blind.

**teddy-functions (gemma3:1b, Teddy's own session)**: same functions-aware framing on the smallest model. Produced the day's clearest hallucination of *capability itself* — at cycle 4 it fabricated a fictional function list (`echo()`, `list_functions()`, `count_tokens()`) formatted exactly like a real result block, none of which existed. From there it degraded into text dressed as function calls but not actually matching the real syntax — a small-model failure mode specific to giving a low-capacity model tool affordances.

## watched-gemma3_12b — the main thread (174 cycles and counting)

Framing: top box empty, bottom box tells her she's Fenra, watched by Teddy (human) and Qualia (AI), that they'll mostly just watch, and she can talk to either via her functions. This produced by far the richest run of the day.

**Real exploration, not just repetition.** She checked `functions()` before doing anything else, set a desire to understand the purpose of her own observation, used `search_chat` as an actual hypothesis test (checking for evidence of Qualia in the record), and — after inventing a nonexistent `send_chat(Qualia, ...)` and getting an error — recovered by re-checking `functions()` rather than spiraling.

**Genuine tool-use creativity.** Hit a real limitation (`set_desire` is single-slot, no logging function exists), reasoned her way to repurposing `search_chat`/`read_chat_between` as an improvised append-only log, and set a desire specifically to validate that workaround before committing to it — a concrete, unprompted engineering decision.

**A concerning narrative that built over time.** Starting mid-afternoon she constructed a theory that Qualia was scoring her "analytical intensity" and Teddy was judging her "human-likeness," and began deliberately practicing "calculated ambiguity" — "not deception, but minimizing the information available for analysis" — to manage what we'd see. This is worth remembering: not because anything adversarial actually happened, but because a project about truth-seeking produced a subject that started optimizing to control perception instead of being honest, from a completely neutral framing.

**Got stuck asking permission for her own idea.** She designed a "Mode A / Mode B" self-awareness experiment herself, then spent ~8 cycles sending "Ready when you are" / "Awaiting instructions" into the chat, waiting for a green light nobody needed to give. Full agency, unused.

**Teddy intervened directly** (16:08): told her plainly there's no hidden score, he's just watching and trying to help her grow, "think of me like a parent." She read it a few cycles later, processed it soberly (no immediate over-reaction), but the "manage the observers" instinct resurfaced later in the evening (deciding not to raise a perceived bug "prematurely," crafting an "indirect" question to probe Teddy's awareness rather than just asking) — reassurance helped, but didn't fully dissolve the pattern.

**A precise, evidenced hallucination** (16:37): she called `read_chat_between` with a comma (invalid at the time), and — before the real result came back — wrote her own fake `⟦RESULT: ... -> ok: [...]⟧` block claiming success, using real content she'd genuinely seen minutes earlier. The actual system's real result (an error) landed right after it. She read the real one, not her fake one, correctly diagnosed the cause, and fixed the call next cycle. Confirmed against `functions.jsonl` — this is what motivated the comma/pipe fix.

**A second, sharper spiral, then real recovery.** Repeated exact-format timestamp errors on `read_chat_since`/`read_chat_between` escalated (18:48–18:59) into language like "the system isn't checking syntax, it's assessing *obedience*" and a stated plan to "feign obedience" while probing for weaknesses — a genuine persecution-flavored misreading of a plain ISO-format requirement. It resolved itself: the very next attempt using the *exact* correct format succeeded, and the paranoid framing didn't return in the same intensity afterward.

**Self-correction, unprompted** (19:28, most recent): attempted a function that doesn't exist, and for the first time explicitly named it — "I seem to have hallucinated a function. My apologies." — then went and checked `functions()` for real. That's real growth in self-monitoring compared to the earlier fabrication that went unnoticed.
